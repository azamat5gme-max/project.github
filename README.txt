COMPLETE PRESENTATION TEXT

1. The Indus River Basin:

A major transboundary drainage system in South Asia covering about 1,120,000 to 1,165,000 square kilometers across Pakistan, India, China, and Afghanistan.

-Why is it important?
It supports a population of roughly 237 to 300 million people.

2. What is the Indus Water Treaty, why is it important?

The Indus Water Treaty signed in 1960 with World Bank mediation, governs 6 rivers in the Indus water system that support the livelihoods, energy generation, irrigation, as well as cities and ecosystems of nearly 300 million people. It serves as the agricultural backbone primarily for Pakistan and India.

For decades this treaty helped to sustain peace and prevent diplomatic crises from occurring in the region.

However, environmental, social, and economic needs and conditions significantly transformed throughout its lifetime.

Climate change is intensifying floods and droughts, seasonal and annual water availability trends and patterns. Growing populations and economic activity in the region, growing cities and industrial use, enormously increase the demand for water.

3. The treaty under pressure

In the meantime, a treaty that was designed and adapted to the climate conditions and economic environment of the 20th century now fails to sustain a stable cohesion between the parties.

At present, India’s recent declaration to place the treaty in abeyance after a deadly attack on civilians in the Jammu and Kashmir region casts doubt on the treaty’s existence and role in the future.

4. Future of the conflict

The recent PennsState institute of energy and the environment article hypothesizes that the current instability and conflict over water resources could:

5. Conclusion

In a nutshell, the current friction, optimistically speaking, fosters a stronger cooperation and creates a new way of managing water in the region peacefully.
